MEMORANDUM

TO: Supervising Attorney
FROM: Avery Example
RE: Liability of Swift Parcel Co. for the bicycle-rack injury

QUESTION PRESENTED

Is Swift Parcel Co. likely to be liable in negligence when its driver's sudden stop caused a package to fall from a bicycle rack on a car two vehicles behind, injuring that car's passenger?

SHORT ANSWER

Probably not. The passenger was likely outside the zone of foreseeable danger created by the sudden stop, so a court following Palsgraf would find no duty.

DISCUSSION

A defendant owes a duty only to plaintiffs within the zone of reasonably foreseeable danger. Palsgraf v. Long Island R.R. Co., 248 N.Y. 339, 162 N.E. 99 (1928). In Palsgraf the court found no liability because the injury to a woman standing far down the platform was not a foreseeable result of the guards' conduct. Here, a sudden stop foreseeably endangers the car directly behind, but a package falling from a rack on a car two vehicles back is a far less predictable consequence.

The plaintiff will argue that duty extends broadly to anyone foreseeably affected by careless driving. MacPherson v. Buick Motor Co., 217 N.Y. 382, 111 N.E. 1050 (1916), held that a manufacturer owes a duty to foreseeable users even without privity of contract. But MacPherson concerned a dangerous product, not a chain reaction on the road, so it does not stretch the zone of danger here.

Courts have also held that delivery companies are strictly liable for any roadway accident involving their vans. Hartwell v. Simmons Grocery Co., 412 F.3d 1187 (9th Cir. 2005). If that rule applied, Swift Parcel would be liable regardless of foreseeability.

CONCLUSION

Under Palsgraf, the passenger was probably outside the foreseeable zone of danger, so Swift Parcel is likely not liable.
